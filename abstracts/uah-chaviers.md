Infection with the hepatitis B virus (HBV) causes long-term harm to the liver, often resulting in cirrhosis, liver failure, and liver cancer. HBV is transmitted via fluid contact, and a particular concern is transmission in childbirth. Vaccination can prevent infection, and is over 95% effective, but vaccination is not universal. In this talk, we provide a brief overview of a SEICR-type epidemiological model for the disease. We investigate various aspects of the model that are of interest in more general investigations of dynamical systems (e.g. well-posedness, equilibria, stability), and explain their application to our model. We follow up with a control-theoretic analysis on the role of vaccination in our model, and pre-empt questions for future research into the control of HBV and the mathematical role of vertical transmission.
